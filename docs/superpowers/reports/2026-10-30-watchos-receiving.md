## The rules as built

- **Ruling 50, the receivers' rules, ported and held to the vectors.** `testTheReceiversRulesAreTheContractsVectors` runs every `contract` case: 6 fingerprints, 19 deliveries (3 read, 16 refused: the empty echo, a bare fingerprint, 0, a sign, a leading zero, a space, an extra or missing segment, capitals, one past `Long.MAX_VALUE`, an overflow), 10 stores and 3 echoes, and `FeedStore.steps` equals `storeSteps`. The number is read as `Int64`.
- **The store, in `STORE_STEPS`' order:** a delivery that `stores` refuses is `.stale` before the text is read (a newer or a broken text under a stale delivery is stale too, and leaves no notice). Then `.malformed` keeps the feed and its delivery; `.newerVersion` keeps them and writes `feed.newer`, naming the version. Otherwise the delivery file is removed, the feed written to `feed.json.tmp`, synced and renamed over `feed.json`, the delivery written the same way (a failure there leaves none and does not fail the write), and the notice cleared. A feed with no delivery (the DEBUG samples) is stored whatever is held, its delivery removed first.
- **`.unchanged`** is the same fingerprint as the delivery it replaces, with a feed held and no notice to clear. Only `.accepted` reloads the widgets; since review round 2, so does a `.newerVersion` whose notice has just come.
- **Backup (Ruling 39):** the store marks its directory `isExcludedFromBackup` whenever it opens it, so a directory an earlier build made is marked too. The feed, the delivery, the notice and the stamp all live in it (`testEveryFileLivesInTheExcludedDirectory`). That is the watch's half of the policy's Backups paragraph.
- **The handshake:** `[refresh: echoOf(<the delivery held>)]`, and "" while no feed is held. Ruling 39: the watch sends nothing else. `HandshakePolicy`: never twice within 60 s (a stamp ahead of the clock holds nothing back, as the Wear policy's); `activated` and `reachable` always; `opened` only with no feed or one that `needsRefresh`. `WatchSession` stamps a handshake as it goes and takes the stamp back if `sendMessage` fails. The stamp keeps its time inside `refresh.asked`, never in the file's date, so no file-timestamp API is used.
- **The drain:** an intake is in flight from the start of `accept` until its write and, on `.accepted` (and since review round 2 on a newly noticed newer format), `reloadAllTimelines()` have returned. `drain()` returns when `ConnectivityDrain.isDrained` holds, checked after each delegate callback (a callback wakes it) and every 0.1 s, or after 10 s, and logs `TaqwaWatch drain <drained|limit> <ms> ms`. At activation the session drains first, then shakes hands. Since review round 1 it also returns as soon as watchOS cancels the background task, logging `cancelled`.
- **Logs (Ruling 52 item 4):** every line is `NSLog("%@", …)`: `TaqwaWatch accepted 5/fedcba9876543210 31 days via context`, `TaqwaWatch stale <seq>/<fp> via activation`, `TaqwaWatch handshake activated have=<seq>/<fp>`, `TaqwaWatch handshake activated answered queued=true`, and so on. No line names the origin, the city or the bearing (`testALogLineNamesTheNumberAndFingerprintNeverTheOrigin`). Errors are logged by type, never by a description that could carry a path.
- **The DEBUG launch arguments** (`DebugArguments`, applied by `DebugLaunch` before the session starts): `-taqwaNoFeed 1` clears the feed, delivery and notice; `-taqwaSampleFeed capetown|en12|ar-ly12|ar-sa` stores that sample moved by whole days so its 18 October is today in Cape Town (UTC+2 all year), every instant moved and a midnight of 0 left 0, with `-taqwaPhrase`'s phrase (`debug-phrase`, target 33) after its own; `-taqwaNewerSeen 1` then offers the store `{"version":2}`, so the notice is the one a newer phone leaves; `-taqwaAt HH:MM` starts the clock at the moved 18 October's midnight plus that time, ticking, and `-taqwaFrozen 1` stops it; `-taqwaPage` is parsed for Task 7.

## Where it differs from the plan

Written into plan 4 Task 5 as an as-built note:

1. `FeedStore.write` and `FeedIntake.accept` are `throws`. The plan's signatures return a result alone, but a file system error putting the feed in place has no honest result: the Wear store throws its `IOException`. A failed delivery write is still swallowed, as the plan says.
2. `.unchanged` also needs a readable feed held and no notice to clear. The plan's rule alone would skip the widget reload when a notice goes away, though the surfaces draw the notice (`testTheSameFeedAfterANewerNoticeIsAccepted`).
3. The notice names its version, as the Wear store's does, so an updated watch says nothing more. That needed `FeedDecoder.versionOf`.
4. The handshake echoes "" while no feed is held, as the Wear app's `PhoneAsk.payload` does, so a delivery file left without its feed is never echoed.
5. The launch arguments' rules live in `WatchCore/DebugSample.swift` (`#if DEBUG`), so `swift test` holds them (13 tests). The plan named only the app's `DebugLaunch.swift`, which now applies them.
6. Until Task 7, the root shows the feed's city when a feed is held and no notice is seen.

## Review round 1, and its fix

Two reviewers, 13:25 to 13:39 that day: the code lens (code quality, platform APIs, tests, privacy, accessibility and hygiene) found 0 Critical, 2 Important and 10 minors; the spec lens (spec, behaviour and evidence) found 0 Critical, 1 Important and 6 minors.

1. **`drain()` ignored the background task's cancellation (code lens, Important): FIXED.** Apple's `Scene.backgroundTask(_:action:)` reference says the system cancels the task when the action has not returned in time, and points to `withTaskCancellationHandler` to observe that. The old loop looked only at its 10 s limit, and its pause, a bare `withCheckedContinuation`, is never woken by a cancellation. So a session that had not drained when watchOS ran out of time kept the closure alive for up to 10 s past the budget, risking termination and a smaller budget at the next wake. Now:
   - `ConnectivityDrain.end(drained:cancelled:elapsed:)` is the stop rule: `.drained` first (said even when the task was cancelled too, since the work is done), then `.cancelled`, then `.limit` at 10 s.
   - `ConnectivityDrain.wait(until:waiter:)` is the loop, reading `Task.isCancelled` at every look, and `DrainWaiter` its pauses. A cancellation handler wakes a pause at once, and a pause begun in a task already cancelled returns at once (the handler then ran before there was anything to wake).
   - `WatchSession.drain()` is that wait, and logs `TaqwaWatch drain <drained|cancelled|limit> <ms> ms`. Its own waiter fields went into `DrainWaiter`, which keeps the same wakes: each delegate callback and each intake's end.
   - **Evidence:** `ConnectivityDrainTests` gains 13 tests (17 in all). Seven of them run real tasks: a wait cancelled after 100 ms with a 5 s poll ends `.cancelled` in under a second, as does one in a task cancelled before it began; a wake ends a pause and the wait looks again; the limit ends a wait on time. `swift test` gives 81 tests, 0 failures, and the drain tests were green 15 times in a row. Seven mutants of `ConnectivityDrain.swift`, run in a scratch copy, were all killed: the pre-fix pause without the handler, `end` ignoring cancellation, no cancellation check inside the pause, the wait never passing cancellation, cancelled said over drained, `>` for `>=` at the limit, and a `wakeAll` that drops its waiters (that one hangs, as a lost continuation would, and was killed by its timeout).

## Review round 2, and its fix

A `.newerVersion` wrote the notice and told the app alone. Task 6 builds the complications' empty form from `update = newerVersionSeen`, and with no feed, or a spent one, that form is one entry with no `reloadAt` (its policy `.never` with no feed). So a fresh or spent watch paired with a newer phone kept "Open Taqwa on your phone" on its complications until the watch app was updated, the case Review Focus 1 names ("with a feed or without").    - `FeedIntake.reloadsWidgets(_:noticeBefore:noticeAfter:)` is the rule, pure: `.accepted` always; `.newerVersion` only when the notice is on disk after the intake and was not before; `.unchanged`, `.stale`, `.malformed` and nothing to store never.

   - So the reloads stay within WidgetKit's budget. A newer feed's delivery is not stored, so the watch's echo still names the older one and the phone may send the newer feed again at each handshake; each finds the notice there and reloads nothing. A newer format of another version says the same thing and reloads nothing. A notice whose file could not be written is not there for the widgets to read, so it reloads nothing either (the app still says it, for as long as its process lives).
   - `FeedIntake.take(_:into:)` is `accept` with the notice read just before and just after it, and gives `FeedIntake.Outcome(result:reloadsWidgets:)`. `WatchSession`'s intake calls it and reloads on `reloadsWidgets`, so the order of the reads is held by `swift test` too, not left to the app.
