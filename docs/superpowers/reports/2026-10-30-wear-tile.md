## Step 0: the renderer's quota (erratum p3 #6)

The plan's countdown is plan 1's `CountdownExpression.countdown`, with `CountdownExpression.spoken` as the line's speech. Erratum p3 #6 said a Tile renderer charges dynamic values against a fixed quota. The spike measured it on emulator-5558 (renderer schema 1.500, Wear OS 6), with throwaway Tiles that were never committed (`SpikeTileA`–`H`, removed from the tree and from the watch):

| Tile | Each Text's dynamic string | Bound of 10 |
|---|---|---|
| C | 10 conditions on distinct instants: 50 operations, 31 fixed values | 4 |
| D | the same, one duration repeated in all 10 | 4 |
| E | 20 conditions on a constant: 20 operations, 41 fixed values | 10 |
| F | 21 such conditions | 9 |
| G | 24 such conditions | 8 |
| A | plan 1's countdown for the night: 380 operations (663 nodes with the fixed values) | 0 |
| B | A with plan 1's spoken sentence beside it: 796 operations | 0 |

So a Tile's dynamic values share **200 nodes**. A fixed value costs nothing, every operation costs one, and a repeated part of an expression is not shared (D binds what C binds). Over the quota the renderer logs `DynamicDataPipeline: … Dynamic type expression limit reached` and keeps the static value: a countdown that never moves. The protolayout-expression-pipeline 1.4.2 the app links charges the same way (`DynamicDataNode.FIXED_NODE_COST` is 0). Tile H showed that a countdown within the quota moves: a 108-node chain moved minute by minute with the app's process killed (`run-as world.taqwa.app kill -9`, then `pidof` empty; `am kill` does not end it while a Tile service is bound).

## The design that fits

- **The countdown and its sentence are chains of the rules' own words** (`TileCountdown`). Each link means "while no more than m minutes are left, say the words for m", the words being `WatchRules.countdown`'s or `spokenCountdown`'s for that minute. A link is five operations (the condition, its comparison, and the minutes since the start read off the platform's clock) and three fixed values (the start, the minutes, the words): eight nodes as Google counts them. The words all the links share are said once around the chain (`sharedEnds`, which never splits a surrogate pair). Every language, digit set and plural is the rules' by construction.
- **Entries are ten minutes of the time left** (`TilePlan.STEP_SECONDS`): cut at 15:00, 15:10 and so on before a start at 16:10, and since fix round 2 at 16:08, the last two minutes being an entry of their own (`LAST_MINUTE_SECONDS`), as well as at every time of the day, every start and every turn of the date. Each chain covers its entry and half a minute's margin either side (`MARGIN_SECONDS`, a minute before review fix round 1), for a renderer that changes entries a little late or early: twelve minutes, so eleven links. The heaviest entry costs 188 of the 200 nodes counting every node, its fixed values too, and one more for each binding (fix round 1; the emulator's own count, the operations alone, makes it far less).
- **The countdown moves for the timeline's first three hours** (`MOVING_SECONDS`). Every entry carries its whole layout, and an hour of ten-minute entries weighs about 35 KB of the one binder call the Tile is sent in, so ten periods of moving countdown would be about 1.7 MB. Past three hours each entry draws the line in call 6's form, the name and the start's time ("ASR 16:10", said "Asr, 16:10"), which stays true for as long as it is shown. The timeline still reaches ten periods and ends in the notice. After fix round 1, over 48 hours of requests (every hour at :00, :13 and :37) in the four samples, the phone's Urdu and Tromsø, with and without the update, the heaviest Tile is 186 KB, the Urdu with the update (`MAX_TIMELINE_BYTES` is 200 KB).
- **Fresh for two hours at most** (`MAX_FRESH_MILLIS`). That is within Ruling 22's six, and an hour short of the moving part, so the system asks for the Tile again while it still moves. A request it misses leaves a Tile that says the time, never a stale countdown.
