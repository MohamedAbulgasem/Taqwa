Four styles, one face

One face on the Galaxy Watch, its style picked in the watch's face settings. All four put the time and the date first, and each has its own always-on design, drawn under it.

  • Always-on stays under Google's limit of 15% of the screen lit: thin strokes and no seconds, with the outline and the countdown kept.
  • Every style follows the phone's 12- or 24-hour setting. Try the Clock switch above.
  • The Taqwa arch sits above the dates on Ring and Sun and at twelve on Moon, where a watchmaker puts the maker's mark.
  • The face holds no code. The prayer, the countdown and the arc come from the Taqwa app on the watch through fixed slots, so the face never works out a time of its own. The Moon's phase is the watch's own.

Through the day

The style picked above at six moments. The arc runs from the last prayer that has begun to the next, as the app's ring does, so after sunrise it still counts from Fajr to Dhuhr.

In the phone's language

The watch takes its language from the phone, and its digits from the phone's region, as the app does: Western digits in Libya and the Maghreb, Arabic-Indic in Saudi Arabia and Egypt.

  • Arch lights from the crown down both sides at once, so it reads the same either way. Ring fills anticlockwise in Arabic, as the app's ring does. Sun and Moon are clocks, so they turn clockwise in every language, and so does the tasbeeh ring, which follows the bezel.
  • On a Friday, Dhuhr reads Jumuʿah on every screen, as in the app.
  • French, Turkish, Indonesian, Bengali and Urdu use the same layout. The new phrases ("in 1 h 12 min", the bezel and crown hints) go to native review with the rest.
  • The Arabic countdown is drawn short, بعد 1 س 12 د. The long form would be بعد ساعة و12 دقيقة. Your call, decision 6.

On faces people already wear

Apple doesn't let apps make faces, so on the Apple Watch Taqwa lives in the slots of Apple's own faces. On the Galaxy the same complications work on Samsung's faces, for anyone who keeps theirs.

  • Every Taqwa complication shows the same thing: how much of the time between two prayers has passed, as a ring or a bar. Only the Sun style draws the whole day.
  • Some Apple faces tint complications in the face's own colour. The shapes are drawn to read in one colour too.
  • Outside the app the countdown reads "in 1 h 12 min". Inside it, the app's ticking 1:11:36 stays, as on the phone.

One swipe from the face

The Smart Stack on the Apple Watch and a Tile on the Galaxy show the day: the current prayer lit as in the app's timeline, and the next one counted down.

The watch app

Three screens, in this order: today's times, tasbeeh and Qibla. A swipe moves between them on both watches, so on the tasbeeh screen the bezel and the crown are free to count.

Today

Tasbeeh

Counted the way the phone counts. After prayer runs 33 · 33 · 34 straight through to 100, with a notch at 33 and 66. Every other phrase counts to 100. Each count ticks, the end of each part gives a short buzz, 100 a long one, then the next round begins. The notches at 33 and 66 show once the ring has passed them, as on the phone.

27 of 100 · SubhanAllah

Try it: tap the watch or press Space to count. Turn the mouse wheel or use the arrow keys for the bezel: forward counts one, back takes one away.

Qibla

The app's dial. It turns with the watch's compass, the Kaaba marker sits at 23° on the rim, and when you face it the rim lights with a buzz. The direction comes from the phone's location, so the watch asks for no location of its own. Both your test watches have a compass.

Your picks, and what's left

Built in from your picks

  1. Quiet Ring as the base, with the time and date first. Ring is that face; Sun keeps the Sun Path; Arch carries the same progress in the icon's own arch.
  2. A Taqwa face on the Galaxy, and complications on both watches. As drawn.
  3. Tasbeeh counts like the phone. 33 · 33 · 34 after prayer, 100 for the rest, with the bezel and the crown.
  4. Qibla: the dial. The app's own.
  5. Each watch in its phone's language. And its digits.
  6. The countdown reads "in 1 h 12 min". On the face, the complications, the Smart Stack and the Tile.

For you to decide

  1. Which styles v1 ships, and which one the watch starts on: your Ring or my Arch. My lean: Arch, Ring and Sun, starting on Arch, and Moon too if this pass wins you over. The real test is wearing Arch and Ring for a day each on your Galaxy; I can put both on it.
  2. Colours: amber only, or amber with Sage and Night, and Pearl as the one-colour choice. My lean: all four, amber first.
  3. Your own complications: two slots along the bottom, one on Moon, empty until you add them. My lean: yes.
  4. Turning the bezel or the crown back takes the last count away. The phone has no undo, but a bezel gets bumped. My lean: yes.
  5. Your own tasbeeh phrases from the phone on the watch too. My lean: yes, they come across with the times.
  6. The Arabic countdown: بعد 1 س 12 د as drawn, or بعد ساعة و12 دقيقة. Yours to call.
  7. An "Add to Apple Watch" button in the iPhone app that hands over one of Apple's faces with Taqwa already in its slots. Apple calls this face sharing; I'd check it in the spec. My lean: yes.

Tried and left out: bending the ring itself into an arch. On a round screen it read as a misshapen ring. The icon's own arch, standing on the screen's edge, works where that didn't.
